# Lervit Enterprise Partner Portal PRD — OOMovers Pilot (v1.0)

## 1. Executive Summary

Lervit is building an enterprise partner portal to operationalize pilot fulfillment with OOMovers and establish a scalable model for future enterprise logistics partners. The portal will allow partner organizations to onboard into Lervit, configure service coverage and dispatch readiness, receive and manage bookings, assign teams and vehicles, update fulfillment statuses, and report incidents — all while preserving Lervit as the customer-facing platform.

This product matters now because Lervit has moved beyond informal partnership discussions into active pilot execution. Manual coordination through email, calls, or messaging may work for a few bookings, but it does not provide the consistency, auditability, visibility, or customer experience required for a reliable marketplace. At the same time, moving directly into a full API integration would be premature at this stage because workflow details, operational ownership, and partner technical readiness still need validation.

A portal-first approach is the right move because it gives Lervit a fast and controlled way to launch the enterprise workflow, validate real operational requirements, and standardize backend processes before automating them more deeply. The portal will be built as a partner-generic interface on top of the same shared backend used by Lervit’s customer-facing product, ensuring compatibility with future native mobile apps and later API-based enterprise integrations.

## 2. Background and Problem Statement

### Background

Lervit is an AI-powered micro-logistics platform focused on small-load and short-haul moving. The company has signed a term sheet with OOMovers, an enterprise moving partner, and needs a practical way to operationalize the relationship.

### Problem Statement

Lervit needs a structured enterprise operations layer that supports pilot fulfillment without creating fragmented workflows, technical debt, or customer confusion.

Current challenges include:

* Manual handoff methods such as phone, email, and messaging do not scale.
* There is no standardized partner-side workflow for booking acceptance, dispatch readiness, status updates, or incident handling.
* Lervit still needs visibility and control over enterprise-fulfilled jobs because customers book through Lervit, not directly through the partner.
* Full direct API integration is too early because the exact workflow, data requirements, and partner technical capabilities still need to be validated in real pilot operations.
* Without a structured portal, Lervit risks poor customer tracking, inconsistent status updates, weak accountability, and operational ambiguity.

The partner portal must solve this by enabling enterprise onboarding, booking coordination, dispatch workflow management, status synchronization, and exception handling while preserving a clean architecture that supports future native apps and API-driven partner integrations.

## 3. Goals and Objectives

### Business Goals

* Launch an enterprise-ready pilot workflow for OOMovers.
* Reduce friction in the booking handoff process between Lervit and enterprise partners.
* Preserve Lervit as the primary customer-facing brand and booking platform.
* Establish a repeatable enterprise-partner model for future partners.
* Improve customer confidence through visible enterprise booking statuses.

### User Goals

* Enable partners to onboard quickly with clear setup steps.
* Enable dispatch and operations teams to manage Lervit jobs without email back-and-forth.
* Give Lervit admins operational visibility over partner-handled jobs.
* Give customers meaningful visibility into enterprise booking progress.

### Technical Goals

* Standardize booking states, operational events, and partner-side actions in the backend.
* Build the portal on the same shared backend / API layer as the current Lervit system.
* Avoid OOMovers-specific hardcoding and ensure future multi-partner reusability.
* Create a foundation for future API integration and native app evolution.

## 4. Non-Goals

The following are explicitly out of scope for v1:

* Full direct API integration with OOMovers at launch.
* Complex GPS fleet telematics integration.
* Enterprise ERP, invoicing, or accounting system integration.
* Deep analytics or BI suite beyond basic operational dashboards.
* Full driver-level HR, payroll, or compliance lifecycle management.
* Customer-facing redesign of the main Lervit booking flow.
* Full live map tracking for enterprise jobs in the first release.
* Multi-language support unless legally or commercially required.

## 6. Key Use Cases

## 8. Booking Operations Workflow

### Workflow Overview

1. Booking is created in Lervit.
2. Lervit determines that the booking should be routed to an enterprise partner.
3. Booking appears in partner portal inbox.
4. Partner reviews booking details.
5. Partner accepts or rejects job.
6. If accepted, partner assigns driver/team/vehicle.
7. Partner updates operational statuses as fulfillment progresses.
8. If something goes wrong, partner flags an incident or delay.
9. On completion, partner uploads proof of completion.
10. Booking state syncs back into Lervit for customer visibility and internal operations.

### Recommended Booking Status Model

| Status             | Meaning                                    | Customer Visible?            |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------- |
| New                | Booking routed to partner, awaiting action | No                           |
| Under Review       | Partner reviewing booking details          | Optional                     |
| Accepted           | Partner has accepted booking               | Yes                          |
| Rejected           | Partner declined booking                   | No                           |
| Assigned           | Driver/team/vehicle assigned               | Optional                     |
| En Route to Pickup | Team is traveling to pickup                | Yes                          |
| Arrived at Pickup  | Team has arrived                           | Yes                          |
| Picked Up          | Items collected                            | Yes                          |
| In Transit         | Delivery underway                          | Yes                          |
| Arrived at Dropoff | Team reached destination                   | Yes                          |
| Delivered          | Delivery completed                         | Yes                          |
| Completed          | Booking fully closed with completion proof | Yes                          |
| Delayed            | Booking impacted by delay                  | Yes                          |
| Issue Reported     | Problem flagged requiring attention        | Yes, with simplified wording |
| Cancelled          | Booking cancelled                          | Yes                          |

### Assumption

The first version will prioritize status-based tracking and ETA windows, not continuous live map GPS.

## 13. Architecture Requirements

The partner portal must be implemented as a separate partner-facing interface on top of the same shared backend used by Lervit’s customer-facing product.

### Architecture Principles

* Customer web/PWA, partner portal, and future native apps must consume shared backend services.
* Business rules such as booking status logic, assignment validation, notification mapping, and incident state handling must live in the backend.
* The portal should only represent partner-facing workflows and UI actions, not duplicate domain logic.
* Partner-specific configurations should live in data/configuration layers, not hardcoded application logic.
* The architecture must support future addition of multiple enterprise partners without per-partner rewrites.

### High-Level Architecture Narrative

* **Frontend clients:** Lervit customer app/PWA, partner portal, future native apps
* **Shared backend services:** auth, booking orchestration, partner management, status engine, incident management, notification service
* **Persistence layer:** shared database with organization-scoped partner models
* **Future integration layer:** APIs and webhooks for enterprise automation

## 17. Risks and Mitigations

| Risk                         | Description                                                | Mitigation                                                 |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Overbuilding too early       | Portal becomes too complex for pilot needs                 | Strict MVP scope control                                   |
| Partner technical immaturity | OOMovers may not have mature internal systems              | Portal-first approach, avoid dependency on partner APIs    |
| Unclear dispatch ownership   | Ambiguity around who updates what                          | Define workflow ownership in ops playbook and UI roles     |
| Inconsistent status updates  | Partner may fail to update statuses reliably               | Strong status prompts, SLA expectations, audit logs        |
| Customer confusion           | Customer may not understand enterprise-fulfilled job state | Clear customer-facing status mapping and support messaging |
| Hardcoded partner logic      | Build becomes OOMovers-specific                            | Use configuration and partner-scoped models                |
| Native roadmap drift         | Portal evolves separately from core system                 | Shared backend and service architecture                    |

## 20. Acceptance Summary for MVP

The MVP will be considered successful if:

* OOMovers can be invited, onboarded, and activated through the portal.
* OOMovers can receive, accept/reject, assign, and update bookings without email-based primary workflow dependence.
* Customer-facing status visibility is preserved inside Lervit.
* Lervit can audit booking actions and incident events.
* The portal runs on shared backend services without creating a separate business logic stack.
* The system is reusable for future enterprise partners with minimal rework.

## 21. Assumptions

* OOMovers is willing to operate through a portal during the pilot.
* Lervit remains the customer-facing booking platform throughout the pilot.
* Full GPS tracking is not required for initial pilot launch.
* The first version prioritizes operational control and visibility over deep automation.
* Future native migration will reuse the same backend services rather than replace them.

## 22. Implementation Recommendation

Proceed with a lean but structured portal MVP focused on onboarding, booking operations, status control, and issue handling. Keep the product partner-generic, backend-centric, and configuration-driven. Use real pilot behavior to inform which workflows should later become APIs rather than prematurely engineering a deep integration.
